10 Best Things to Do on a Phuket Yacht Join Trip

If you’ve been dreaming of turquoise water, hidden lagoons, and a day spent hopping between limestone islands without the hassle of chartering a private boat, a Phuket yacht join trip is exactly what you’re looking for. A “join trip” simply means you share a boat with other travelers rather than booking the entire vessel, which makes it one of the most affordable and social ways to experience Phuket’s coastline from the water.

Whether you’re a solo traveler, a couple on honeymoon, or a small group of friends, a join trip on a catamaran or speedboat gives you access to snorkeling reefs, secluded beaches, and dramatic karst islands that are simply out of reach for land-based tourists. Below, we’ve rounded up the ten best experiences you can expect on a Zenith Yacht Charters join trip, along with practical tips to help you plan the perfect day at sea.

1. Snorkeling at Coral Island (Koh Hey)

Coral Island, known locally as Koh Hey, is one of the most popular first stops on any Phuket join trip. Just a short sail from Chalong Pier, the island is famous for its calm, shallow bays and healthy coral gardens that are ideal for beginners and experienced snorkelers alike. Expect to see parrotfish, barracuda, trevally, and colorful reef life cruising through crystal-clear water. Most join trips anchor here for an hour or two, giving you time to swim, relax on the white sand, or simply enjoy the view from the boat’s sundeck before continuing on the next stop.

2. Island Hopping Around Racha Yai and Racha Noi

No Phuket yacht join trip itinerary is complete without a stop at the Racha Islands. Racha Yai is known for its postcard-perfect bays and some of the clearest water in the region — visibility can exceed 20 meters on a good day. Racha Noi, slightly further out, is quieter and better suited to travelers hoping to spot larger marine life during the right season. Many operators include a stop near both islands as separate itineraries in combination with other closer islands, so you get variety without extra cost.

3. A Day Trip to the Phi Phi Islands

For travelers with a full day, Phi Phi Island is consistently ranked as the highlight of any Phuket boat trip. The journey takes you past dramatic limestone cliffs and hidden lagoons before reaching Phi Phi Don and Phi Phi Leh. Because the islands sit a couple of hours from Phuket by boat, this stop typically anchors the itinerary of a full-day join trip rather than a half-day excursion.

4. Boardwalk Views (and Restricted Wading) at Maya Bay

Maya Bay, made famous by the film The Beach, sits on Phi Phi Leh and has reopened to visitors with strict conservation limits on boat numbers and time slots — swimming directly in the bay is currently restricted, with entry via a boardwalk instead of anchoring on the sand. Because the rules change seasonally (the bay typically closes for conservation each August and September), it’s worth checking current entry conditions through the Thai National Parks department before you go, or simply asking your join trip crew, who track the latest access windows. The towering cliffs wrapping around the bay still make it one of the most memorable stops in the Andaman Sea.

5. Exploring James Bond Island and Phang Nga Bay

If your join trip route heads north instead of south, you’ll likely pass through Phang Nga Bay, home to the iconic James Bond Island (officially Khao Phing Kan), made famous in The Man with the Golden Gun. James Bond Island itself is a quick stop for photos and viewing rather than swimming, but Phang Nga Bay is where the swimming happens — many join trips that add time here for a dip or a paddle through the mangrove channels and hidden lagoons before continuing. The bay is a maze of these channels and vertical limestone towers rising straight out of the sea, and most routes pair the James Bond Island stop with a scenic cruise past the surrounding karst formations.

6. Sunset Cruise on the Andaman Sea

There’s a reason sunset trips sell out fast. Several join trip departures are timed so the boat is drifting — or gently cruising — as the sun drops behind the Andaman Sea, usually setting off in the afternoon so the golden hour lines up with the final hour on the water. For a more traditional sailing feel, the Oceanland catamaran is an option worth asking about for sunset departures. Onboard drinks and a relaxed atmosphere make this one of the most romantic and low-key ways to end a day on the water, whether you’ve spent the afternoon snorkeling or you’re joining purely for the evening cruise. Many routes time the return leg to pass scenic viewpoints like Promthep Cape or drift near quieter anchorages away from the day-trip crowds, so the water stays calm and the view stays uninterrupted. Because space is limited and demand is high, it’s worth booking a sunset departure a few days ahead, especially during the November–April high season when clear skies make for the best views.

7. Visiting Bamboo Island and the Khai Islands

Less crowded than their famous neighbors, Bamboo Island and the Khai Islands offer a quieter alternative for swimming and beach time. These stops are frequently added as mid-route breaks on longer itineraries, giving guests a chance to walk on soft white sand and snorkel over coral without the density of tour boats found at bigger islands.

8. Deep-Sea Fishing and Water Adventures

For guests who want more than snorkeling, Zenith’s Thailand experiences lineup includes deep-sea fishing trips and scuba excursions alongside the island-hopping routes. Depending on the season and location, you might hook mackerel, barracuda, or trevally, with all tackle and bait typically provided by the crew — a nice way to turn a stop along the route into an active, hands-on part of the day.

9. Water Sports: Snorkeling Gear, Paddleboards, and Beach Time

Boats like the Vice speedboat come equipped with snorkeling gear, a paddleboard, swim floats, and beach chairs at Banana Beach, making them ideal for guests who want an active day on the water without booking separate add-ons. This is a great option for families and groups who want to move between swimming, paddling, and simply relaxing on deck between island stops.

10. A Relaxed Onboard Lunch or Floating Beach Club Day

The onboard dining experience is often underrated. Luxury vessels like the Oceana motoryacht come with a fully equipped kitchen and a large table built for alfresco dining, while guests looking for a livelier atmosphere can add on time at Yona Beach Club, Phuket’s floating oasis with an infinity pool, cabanas, and signature cocktails, or even party while cruising like Noah. Vegetarian and dietary-restricted meal options are usually available on request — just flag this when you book.

More Hidden Gems Worth Adding to Your Itinerary

If you have extra time or are booking a private charter rather than a set-route join trip, a few lesser-known spots are worth building into your route:

  • Maithon Island — a quiet, less-visited island close to the mainland, good for a shorter half-day stop.
  • Naka Island — calm waters on the northeast side of Phuket, popular for its resort beaches.
  • Promthep Cape — a dramatic viewpoint at Phuket’s southern tip, often paired with a sunset cruise departure.
  • Koh Yao Island — a slower-paced island in Phang Nga Bay with traditional fishing villages.
  • Koh Hong Krabi — a hidden lagoon surrounded by limestone cliffs, best reached on a longer full-day charter.

Tips for Planning Your Phuket Yacht Join Trip

  • Book through a licensed operator. Check that your provider carries proper safety equipment and is transparent about pricing — browse the full Zenith yacht fleet for vetted options.
  • Pack reef-safe sunscreen. Marine parks around Phi Phi and Maya Bay restrict standard sunscreens to protect coral.
  • Bring a dry bag. You’ll be moving between the yacht, tender boats, and the water frequently.
  • Confirm the route in advance. Not every join trip visits every island on this list — check the current join trip itinerary before booking.
  • Consider the season. November to April offers the calmest seas and best visibility, while the monsoon season (May–October) can affect certain routes, and Maya Bay closes seasonally for conservation.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a yacht join trip in Phuket?

A yacht join trip is a shared boat tour where you book individual seats rather than the whole vessel, joining other travelers for a day cruise around Phuket’s islands. It’s a more affordable alternative to a private charter while still offering access to snorkeling spots, beaches, and onboard dining.

2. How much does a Phuket yacht join trip cost?

Pricing is per person rather than per boat, making join trips significantly cheaper than a full charter. Package rates vary by boat type and route — check current pricing on the join trip page or contact the team directly for the latest rates.

3. Which islands are included on a typical join trip?

Most itineraries include a combination of Coral Island, Racha Yai, and the Khai Islands for shorter day trips, while longer trips may extend to Phi Phi Island or Phang Nga Bay depending on the operator and season.

4. Is a yacht join trip suitable for non-swimmers?

Yes. Life jackets are provided on every trip, and you’re never required to enter the water. You can still enjoy the scenery, onboard lunch, and island stops from the boat or the shore.

5. What should I bring on a Phuket yacht join trip?

Pack swimwear, a change of clothes, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses, and a dry bag for electronics. It’s worth confirming with your operator whether towels and snorkeling gear are included, as they are on boats like the Vice speedboat.

6. Can I book a yacht join trip for a special occasion?

Absolutely. Many guests time a join trip around sunset. Let the operator know in advance if you’d like anything arranged for a celebration — check the FAQ page for booking policies.
