Getting Around Phuket: Taxis, Grab, Bolt & Scooters

Phuket doesn’t have a metro, and getting from your hotel to the beach, a temple, or the pier for a day on the water can mean weighing up several very different options — each with its own price, convenience, and trade-offs. This guide breaks down the main ways to get around the island: metered taxis, Grab, Bolt, the Phuket Smart Bus, and scooter rentals, so you can pick the right one for each leg of your trip. If your plan includes a day out with Zenith Yacht Charters, a yacht charter company offering both join trips and private luxury yacht charters in Thailand, join trips typically include hotel pickup — but for everything else on your itinerary, here’s how the island’s transport options actually compare.

Taxis in Phuket

Metered taxis exist in Phuket, but in practice most drivers at tourist hubs like the airport, Patong, and major beaches negotiate a flat fare instead of running the meter. It’s worth agreeing on a price before you get in, since fares can vary widely depending on the driver and how busy the area is. Taxis are most useful late at night, when Grab availability can thin out, or when you need a driver who already knows a specific hotel or pier without GPS guidance.

When to Use a Taxi

Taxis work best for short, well-known routes — from your hotel to a nearby beach, or back from a restaurant late in the evening. For anything longer, like an airport transfer, it’s worth comparing the fare against the Smart Bus or Grab first, since taxi rates on those routes tend to be the highest of the four options.

Using Grab in Phuket

Grab is the region’s ride-hailing app and generally the most predictable option on the island — you see the fare before you book, and payment is handled in-app, which avoids the negotiation that comes with street taxis. Coverage is strongest around Patong, Karon, Kata, and Phuket Town, though wait times can stretch during rain or peak traffic hours. It’s worth having a local SIM or eSIM active before you land so you can book a Grab straight from the airport.

Grab vs Taxi

Grab tends to work out cheaper than a negotiated taxi fare for the same route, especially for airport transfers and cross-island trips, and the fixed upfront price removes any guesswork. The trade-off is availability — during storms or very late at night, taxis waiting at a hotel stand may be quicker to find than a Grab car.

Using Bolt in Phuket

Bolt is Grab’s main competitor on the island and works the same way — book in-app, see the fare upfront, and pay by card or cash depending on the driver. Many travelers find Bolt fares run slightly cheaper than Grab for the same route, though the gap varies trip to trip, so it’s worth having both apps installed and comparing prices before you book. Bolt also tends to assign a wider mix of vehicles, including minivans and electric cars, which can be a plus if you’re traveling with extra luggage or a larger group and want more space than a standard sedan.

Bolt vs Grab

Both apps cover similar areas of the island, so it usually comes down to whichever quotes the better fare or has a car available fastest at that moment. Since pricing on both apps can shift with demand, checking both before confirming a ride is an easy way to save a little on longer trips like airport transfers.

The Phuket Smart Bus

The Smart Bus is the most budget-friendly way to cover long distances along the island’s west coast. Route 1 runs from Phuket International Airport down through Bang Tao, Surin, Kamala, Patong, Karon, and Kata to Rawai, with a flat fare of 100 baht regardless of how far you travel — a fraction of what the same route costs by taxi. Route 2 connects Phuket Town’s bus terminal with Patong for 50 baht, and the Dragon Line, a free electric bus, loops around Phuket Old Town. Buses accept cash, QR code payment, and contactless Visa or Mastercard.

Smart Bus Pros and Cons

The upside is clear: a flat 100-baht fare beats a taxi that can run into the thousands of baht for the same airport-to-Rawai route. The downside is timing — buses run on a fixed schedule roughly every hour, traffic can add delay, and the route doesn’t cover Phuket Town, Cherng Talay, or the east coast. It suits travelers with flexible timing and standard luggage more than those on a tight schedule or with young kids in tow.

Renting a Scooter in Phuket

A rented scooter gives you the most independence — no waiting for a ride, no fixed bus schedule, and the freedom to detour to a viewpoint or beach on a whim. Rental shops are common near every major beach town, and daily rates are generally affordable compared to repeated taxi or Grab trips.

What to Check Before You Rent

Always ask for a helmet, check the scooter for existing scratches or damage (photograph it before you ride off), and confirm whether the price includes insurance. An international driving permit is technically required, and Phuket’s traffic — especially around Patong and Chalong in the early evening — takes some getting used to if you’re not experienced on two wheels.

Who Scooters Suit Best

Confident riders who want to string together several stops in a day — say, the Big Buddha, Promthep Cape, and a beach — get the most value out of a scooter. If you’re not comfortable riding one, Grab or a taxi for the same multi-stop day is a safer, if pricier, alternative.

Comparing Your Options at a Glance

  • Taxi: Convenient and available almost everywhere, but negotiate the fare first — generally the priciest option for longer trips.
  • Grab: Upfront pricing and no negotiation, strongest coverage around the main tourist areas, but can be harder to find during storms or very late at night.
  • Bolt: Works much like Grab with upfront pricing, and fares are often slightly cheaper — worth comparing both apps before you book, especially since Bolt sometimes assigns minivans or electric cars.
  • Smart Bus: The cheapest way to cover the west coast corridor at a flat 100 baht, but only one main route and a fixed schedule.
  • Scooter: The most flexible option for multi-stop days, best suited to confident riders comfortable with Phuket traffic.

Airport Transfers: A Quick Cost Comparison

Getting off the island’s airport is usually the first transport decision you’ll make, and the price range across your options is wide. The Smart Bus runs a flat 100 baht from the airport down the west coast to Rawai, making it by far the cheapest option, though it only departs roughly once an hour and doesn’t offer door-to-door service. A shared airport minivan typically runs 150–200 baht per person to destinations like Patong, Karon, or Kata, with a short wait for the van to fill up. A private taxi, Grab, or Bolt is the most convenient door-to-door option but costs noticeably more — often several times the Smart Bus fare, depending on your exact hotel and how far it sits from the main road. If you’re traveling with a lot of luggage, arriving very late at night, or have young children who need a car seat, the extra cost of a private transfer is usually worth it. If you’re on a tighter budget and don’t mind the wait, the Smart Bus or a shared minivan will get you there for a fraction of the price.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the cheapest way to get around Phuket?

The Phuket Smart Bus is the cheapest option for longer distances along the west coast, at a flat 100 baht regardless of how far you travel. For short local trips, walking or a quick Grab ride is usually cheaper than a negotiated taxi fare.

2. Grab or Bolt: Which should I use in Phuket?

Both apps cover similar areas, with the strongest coverage around Patong, Karon, Kata, and Phuket Town. Bolt often quotes slightly cheaper fares and sometimes assigns minivans or electric cars, while Grab tends to have wider driver availability. Both also offer a motorbike option, which is usually the cheapest and fastest way to cut through traffic for a solo rider without luggage, though it’s not the safest or most comfortable choice for longer trips or airport transfers. It’s worth having both installed and comparing the quote before booking, especially for longer trips like an airport transfer.

3. Do taxi meters work in Phuket?

Technically yes, but in practice most taxis at tourist areas negotiate a flat fare instead of running the meter. Agree on a price before getting in, especially for airport transfers or longer cross-island routes.

4. Do I need an international license to rent a scooter in Phuket?

An international driving permit is technically required to legally ride a scooter in Thailand, and it can also affect insurance validity if you’re in an accident without one. Always check with the rental shop and confirm what their insurance covers before signing.

5. How do I get to the pier for a yacht charter or join trip?

Join trips typically include hotel pickup as part of the booking. Private charters don’t usually include transfer as standard, but it can be arranged as an add-on — the cost depends on where you’re staying, so it’s worth confirming when you book.

6. Does the Smart Bus run at night?

No, the Smart Bus generally stops running in the early evening, with the last buses around 8:00–9:00 PM. For anything later, a Grab or taxi is your best option.
