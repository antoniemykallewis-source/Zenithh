Best Night Markets in Phuket: Food, Shopping & Hours

When the sun goes down over the Andaman Sea, Phuket comes alive in a completely different way. The island’s night markets are where locals and travelers mix over grilled seafood, fresh mango sticky rice, and stalls piled with handmade souvenirs. If you’ve spent the day out on the water with Zenith Yacht Charters, Thailand’s go-to name for a luxury yacht charter Thailand experience, a Phuket night market is the perfect way to close out the evening — think of it as the on-land encore to a day of island hopping around Phi Phi Island or Phang Nga Bay.

This guide rounds up the best night markets in Phuket, what to eat and buy at each one, their opening hours, and a few tips on making the most of an evening out after a charter day. Whether you’re island hopping on a join trip with a yacht charter company in Thailand or you’ve chosen to book a luxury yacht charter with a top luxury yacht charter broker in Thailand, these markets are an easy, budget-friendly way to round off your trip.

Why Phuket's Night Markets Are Worth Your Evening

Phuket has around ten major night markets scattered across Phuket Town, Patong, Kata, and Chillva, ranging from huge weekend bazaars to small nightly community markets. They’re not just about bargain shopping — they’re a genuine slice of Thai daily life, where you’ll find grilled seafood sizzling on charcoal, fruit smoothies blended to order, and vendors selling everything from handmade soap to phone cases. After a full day on the water, there’s a relaxed, walkable way to enjoy a good meal and pick up a few souvenirs before heading back to your hotel.

Top Night Markets in Phuket

1. Phuket Walking Street (Lard Yai) — Sunday Only

Held every Sunday evening on Thalang Road in Phuket Town’s Old Town, this is the island’s most atmospheric market. The road closes to traffic and the Sino-Portuguese shophouses light up with string lights, giving it a charm no other market on the island can match. Expect street food, local art, clothing, and some of the best cafés in Phuket lining the same road.

Opening hours: Sundays, roughly 4:00 PM – 9:00/11:00 PM.
Address: Thalang Road, Phuket Town.

2. Naka Weekend Market (Talad Tairod)

The largest night market on the island is held on Wichit Songkhram Road near Central Festival. It’s a sprawling maze of food stalls, antiques, clothes, and quirky finds you won’t see anywhere else — a good pick if you only have one evening free and want maximum variety.

Opening hours: Saturday–Sunday, roughly 4:00 PM – 10:00 PM.

3. Chillva Market

Built from recycled shipping containers with rooftop bars, Chillva has a young, creative, indie feel that’s distinct from Phuket’s older markets. It runs on weekdays too, when most other markets are closed, and is best visited Thursday to Saturday when the flea-market section is in full swing.

Opening hours: Daily, best Thursday–Saturday from around 5:00 PM.

4. Patong Night Market

Right in the heart of Phuket’s busiest tourist strip, this market is convenient if you’re staying near Patong Beach after a day trip to Coral Island or Racha Yai. Expect the usual mix of clothing, souvenirs, and street food, with a livelier, more nightlife-adjacent crowd than the Old Town markets.

5. Karon Temple Market

Located near the Dragon Palace temple in Karon, this is one of the liveliest local bazaars on the island, blending spiritual ambiance with everyday commerce — a good stop if you’re staying in Karon or Kata and want something less touristy than Patong.

6. Rawai Seafood Night Market

Close to the Rawai villas, this market specializes in fresh seafood picked straight from the tanks and cooked to order. It pairs naturally with a day spent near Promthep Cape, just a short drive away.

What to Eat at Phuket's Night Markets

Food is the main draw at every market on this list. Look out for grilled seafood (prawns, squid, and whole fish cooked over charcoal), mango sticky rice, fresh fruit smoothies, fried chicken, and noodles cooked to order in front of you. Prices are usually displayed clearly, and portions tend to be generous. If you’re unsure what something is, most vendors are used to tourists and happy to explain in simple English.

What to Buy: Shopping & Souvenirs

Night markets are the best place on the island to pick up souvenirs with a local touch — handmade soaps, batik clothing, small woodwork, phone accessories, and local snacks to take home. Don’t expect international brands or warranties on electronics; this is all about the local look and one-of-a-kind finds. Bargaining is expected and generally polite — you can usually negotiate a discount of around 5–20% off the first price quoted.

Practical Tips for Visiting

  • Check opening days first — several of the best markets, like Naka and Lard Yai, only run on specific days of the week.
  • Go just after sunset for the best atmosphere, food selection, and cooler evening temperatures.
  • Bring cash — most stalls don’t accept cards.
  • Dress light and comfortable — markets are outdoors and can get warm and crowded.
  • Use a taxi, e-hailing app, or tuk-tuk to get there and back, especially late in the evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the best night market in Phuket?

It depends on what you’re after. Naka Weekend Market is the biggest and most varied, Phuket Walking Street (Lard Yai) is the most atmospheric thanks to its Sino-Portuguese setting, and Chillva Market has the most local, creative feel.

2. What days are Phuket night markets open?

Most run on specific days rather than every night. Naka Market runs Saturday and Sunday, Lard Yai runs Sunday only, and Chillva runs daily but is liveliest Thursday to Saturday. Always check the current schedule before planning your evening around one.

3. Do Phuket night markets accept credit cards?

Rarely. Most stalls are cash-only, so it’s worth carrying enough Thai baht for food and any souvenirs you plan to buy.

4. Is it okay to bargain at Phuket night markets?

Yes, politely bargaining is normal and expected, particularly for clothing, souvenirs, and non-food items. A discount of around 5–20% off the first quoted price is typical.

5. What time should I arrive at a Phuket night market?

Arriving just after sunset, around 5:00–6:00 PM, usually gives you the best combination of daylight for photos, a full range of open stalls, and manageable crowds before things get busy later in the evening.

6. Can I visit a night market after a yacht charter in Phuket?
