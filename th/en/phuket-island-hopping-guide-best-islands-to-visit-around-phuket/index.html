Phuket Island Hopping Guide: Best Islands to Visit Around Phuket

Phuket is a launchpad for dozens of islands across the Andaman Sea, each with its own character — from the dramatic cliffs of Phi Phi to the calm coves of Coral Island and the quiet fishing villages of Koh Yao. With Zenith Yacht Charters based across Phuket, island hopping is easy whether you want a private yacht for the day or a seat on a shared trip with a fixed schedule and price.

What Is Island Hopping in Phuket?

Island hopping means visiting two or more islands in a single trip by boat, usually stopping to swim, snorkel, or walk a beach at each one. Around Phuket, this generally takes one of two forms: a shared “join trip” with a fixed group and schedule; or a private charter with your own boat and a route built around your preferences;

Most trips run from early morning to late afternoon — typically ending before 7 pm — timed around calmer seas and a luscious sunset. A single day usually allows two to three islands if they’re close together, or one destination if it’s further out, like Phi Phi. Multi-day trips are also possible on a private charter, with an overnight stay on Phi Phi Don.

How Far Are These Islands from Phuket?

Travel time depends on your boat’s speed, sea conditions, and departure point — most of our trips depart from piers closest to the islands. As a rough guide:

  • Coral Island: 20 – 45 minutes
  • Maithon Island: 20 – 45 minutes
  • Racha Yai / Racha Noi: 1.5 – 3 hours
  • Phang Nga Bay / James Bond Island: roughly 1.5 hours
  • Phi Phi Islands: 1.5 – 2 hours

Faster boats like the Vice speedboat cut these times down noticeably, which is why it’s the go-to option for combining two islands in a single day. A sailing catamaran like Lemon takes a little longer point-to-point, but suits a more relaxed pace where the sailing itself is part of the experience rather than just a means of transport. Conditions also matter: expect slightly longer transit times and a choppier ride during the May–October monsoon season, particularly on west-facing routes.

Best Islands to Visit Around Phuket

1. Phi Phi Islands

The most famous stop on any Phuket itinerary, thanks to Maya Bay’s dramatic cliffs — the cove made famous by the 2000 film “The Beach.” Expect turquoise water, towering karsts, and — outside early morning — some of the biggest crowds in the region. Most trips also stop at Monkey Beach and Pileh Lagoon, a nearly enclosed bay ringed by cliffs that’s popular for photos and calm-water swimming. Plan a full day here rather than combining it with other stops, since the round trip alone takes 3 – 4 hours. The islands fall under Hat Noppharat Thara–Mu Ko Phi Phi National Park — see the official TAT profile for details, or explore Zenith’s Phi Phi Island guide for what to expect on the water.

Best for: first-timers, photography, snorkeling; Ideal duration: full day

2. Racha Yai & Racha Noi

South of Phuket, calmer and clearer than Phi Phi — a solid pick for snorkeling and diving without the same volume of boat traffic. Racha Yai has soft-sand bays, a scattering of beach clubs, a variety of marina life, and shallow reefs close to shore that suit less confident swimmers. Racha Noi sits a little further out and is quieter still, with better underwater visibility for diving and fishing.

Best for: snorkeling, diving, fishing; Ideal duration: full day

3. Coral Island

The easiest, closest option — calm, shallow water suited to first-timers and non-swimmers, with white sandy beaches, guided water toys packages, restaurants and beach chairs at Banana Beach. It’s a popular add-on to a half-day trip because it’s close enough to combine with a second stop like Racha Yai or Maithon Island without eating into the whole day. See Zenith’s Coral Island guide for details.

Best for: families, short trips, beginners, snorkelling/swimming; Ideal duration: half day

4. James Bond Island & Phang Nga Bay

A completely different landscape of limestone karsts, sea caves, and mangrove channels. James Bond Island (Koh Tapu) is small and can get busy with day-trippers by late morning, but the wider Phang Nga Bay — mostly within Ao Phang Nga National Park — is genuinely striking, with hidden lagoons (“hongs”) accessible by kayak at the right tide. Arrive mid-afternoon if you want to avoid the busiest window. See the TAT overview for more.

Best for: scenery, photography, sea canoeing; Ideal duration: full day

5. Maithon Island

Maithon Island sits just off Phuket’s southeast coast with calm, sheltered water and occasional dolphin sightings nearby during non-peak seasons — a good quick swim or snorkeling stop that doesn’t demand a full day.

Best for: a quick swim or snorkel break, with a chance of spotting dolphins; Ideal duration: half-day

6. Bamboo Island

Bamboo Island, north of Phi Phi Don, offers a quieter alternative to Maya Bay’s crowds if you’re already headed in that direction and want a second stop with better snorkeling and relaxing by the beach but with fewer people.

Best for: snorkeling, relaxing on quieter beaches; Ideal duration: add-on to a Phi Phi trip

Sample One-Day Itinerary

A typical private charter day might run: 10:00 am departure from Chalong Pier, 12:00 pm snorkeling at Coral Island, 1:30 pm lunch on board, 3:00 pm a second stop at Racha Yai, and a 4:30 pm return with a potential stop at Promthep Cape around 6.00 pm. For Phi Phi or Phang Nga Bay, expect the whole day dedicated to that one destination — an early 9:30 am departure helps beat the crowds and the midday heat.

How Much Does Island Hopping Cost in Phuket?

Pricing depends on the boat type and trip duration. As a benchmark, Zenith’s private charter packages for the low season start from roughly ฿15,408 for the Aphrodite sailing catamaran, ฿26,900 for the Aqva speedboat, and ฿70,000 for the Aladin motoryacht — prices scale with boat size, boat type, group size, destination, and duration, with most charters requiring a four-hour minimum. A join trip is priced per person and works out cheaper for solo travelers or couples, with Smile Rainbow from around ฿2,100 per pax. Most packages include a captain, crew, fuel, insurance, life vest, and basic snorkeling gear, but check whether national park fees (typically 300 – 400 THB per adult), food, and van/island transfers are included before booking.

Private Charter vs Join Trip

A private charter gives you a custom route, your own captain and crew, and the boat entirely to yourselves — ideal for groups, celebrations, proposals, or anyone who wants flexible timing rather than a fixed schedule. A join trip is a shared, ticketed day out at a lower per-person cost, run on a set itinerary alongside other travelers — a good fit for solo travelers or couples on a budget who don’t need exclusivity. If you’re undecided, group size is usually the deciding factor: under four people generally leans toward a join trip on cost grounds, while larger groups tend to get better overall value — and more flexibility — from a private charter.

What to Pack for Island Hopping

  • Reef-safe sunscreen — required on several islands to protect coral
  • Sunglasses
  • A dry bag for phones, cameras, and other valuables
  • A light cover-up, rash guard, or hat — the open water offers little shade
  • Aquashoes – to guard against sharp rocks/seabeds and hot sands against the soles of your feet
  • Your own snorkeling gear for hygiene
  • Cash in small denominations for park fees and beach vendors
  • A change of clothes and small towel for the ride back
  • Motion sickness tablets if you’re prone to seasickness

Best Time to Visit

November to April is Phuket’s dry season, with the calmest seas, clearest water, and best visibility — also the busiest and most expensive time to book. May to October brings rougher water on west-facing coasts and a higher chance of rain delaying a trip, though Phang Nga Bay and Koh Yao stay relatively sheltered year-round. December through February is the single busiest stretch, so book further ahead if your dates fall in that window.

Safety and Sustainability Tips

Life jackets are provided and should be worn during transfers and water activity, especially for non-swimmers or children. Check the forecast with your operator on the morning of departure — reputable charters reschedule rather than sail into rough conditions. For sustainability: avoid touching coral, use reef-safe sunscreen, and take rubbish back with you.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the best island to visit near Phuket?

Phi Phi is the top pick for scenery and snorkeling, while Racha Yai suits travelers wanting clearer water with fewer crowds; it may be further away from the mainland. Coral Island or Maithon Island are the best for families and short trips close to shore.

2. Should I book a private charter or a join trip?

A private charter suits groups wanting a custom route, their own crew, and privacy. A join trip is more affordable for solo travelers or couples who prioritise island hopping and are happy to share the boat with others.

3. Where do Zenith's boats depart from?

Zenith’s Phuket fleet departs from different piers depending on the route. Southern itineraries — including trips to Coral Island, the Racha Islands and Phi Phi Islands — typically leave from Chalong Pier or Coco Pier. Northern itineraries, such as routes to Phang Nga Bay, Phi Phi Islands and Koh Yao, typically depart from Royal Phuket Marina, Boat Lagoon, Ao Po Pier, or Ao Po Grand Marina. Your exact departure point depends on which yacht and route you book, so it’s worth confirming with your booking crew ahead of time.

4. Which yacht suits a small group day trip?

The Vice speedboat suits up to 8 guests for fast full-day runs to nearby islands, while Lemon’s wider deck suits relaxed sailing for slightly larger groups. For larger groups or special occasions, other catamarans and luxury yachts in the fleet provide extra seating, entertainment areas, and premium onboard facilities. Ultimately, it is up to your own standards of comfort.

5. Which islands near Phuket are best for families with young children?

Coral Island and Maithon Island both have calm, shallow water and short travel times, making them easier for kids compared to islands slightly further away, like Phi Phi.

6. Do I need to book island hopping tours in advance?

During peak season (December – February), popular routes like Phi Phi sell out days ahead, so booking at least 1 – 2 weeks in advance is recommended. Outside peak season, many join trips can be confirmed with as little as 24 – 48 hours’ notice, though private charters and special requests are best arranged further ahead.
